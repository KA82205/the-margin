How the forecast is made

What goes into the number, why each part counts as much as it does, and how the forecast will sharpen as the election gets closer.

What goes into the number

Four kinds of evidence. Polls and fundamentals set the national number. State models and each state's voting history place the states around it. Betting markets are blended in at the end.

Why these weights?

Every weight was written down before the back-test was scored, with a reason, and then each one was changed to see whether it mattered. Mostly it did not: across every alternative tried, the typical state miss on the last day stayed between and points.

Sources and limits

The Margin is a personal, non-commercial project, rerun every day. Polls come from FiveThirtyEight's archive and the model data The Economist published; polling averages for 1972 to 2016 come from FiveThirtyEight's public data; expert ratings are Cook Political Report's and Sabato's Crystal Ball's; results are official counts. The fundamentals use the Fifty-Two Keys count, and the head-to-head polls, state starting points and presidential approval come from the Tipping Point Nowcast. The code and data are in the repository.

Limits worth knowing: pollsters are not rated, so each counts the same apart from sample size and recency. Betting odds are in the 2028 number but not in the back-tests, because election-eve odds for 2008 to 2024 still have to be collected from sources that can be checked. The Fifty-Two Keys count for a past year uses that year's final data.
